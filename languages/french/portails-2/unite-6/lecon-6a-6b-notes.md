# 3. Demonstrative Adjectives — **ce, cet, cette, ces**

Demonstrative adjectives mean:

> **this / that / these / those**

In French, the form depends on the **gender** and **number** of the noun.

| Form | Use | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ce** | masculine singular before a consonant | **ce livre** | this/that book |
| **cet** | masculine singular before a vowel or silent **h** | **cet homme** | this/that man |
| **cette** | feminine singular | **cette maison** | this/that house |
| **ces** | any plural noun | **ces livres** | these/those books |

## How to choose the right form

Ask yourself:

1. Is the noun **singular or plural**?
2. If singular, is it **masculine or feminine**?
3. If masculine singular, does it begin with a **vowel or silent h**?

## **-ci** and **-là**

Your textbook vocabulary also introduces:

| French | Basic meaning |
|---|---|
| **-ci** | here |
| **-là** | there |

These can appear after a noun to emphasize “this one here” or “that one there.”

### Examples

- **Ce cadeau-ci !** = This present here!
- **Ces tee-shirts-là sont en solde.** = Those T-shirts there are on sale.

> [!tip] Beginner memory trick
> **ci = close** can help you remember “here.”
>
> **là = there** can help you remember something farther away.

# 4. Passé Composé with **avoir**

The **passé composé** is a major French past tense.

Your lesson explains that it is used for **specific actions that started and finished in the past**.

It often corresponds to English:
- simple past: **I ate**
- present perfect: **I have eaten**

---

## Basic Formula

> **Subject + present tense of avoir + past participle**

Example:

> **J’ai mangé une pomme.**  
> I ate / I have eaten an apple.

---

## Present Tense of **avoir**

You need these forms to build the passé composé.

| Subject | Avoir | English |
|---|---|---|
| je / j’ | **ai** | have |
| tu | **as** | have |
| il / elle / on | **a** | has |
| nous | **avons** | have |
| vous | **avez** | have |
| ils / elles | **ont** | have |

### Put it together

| Subject | Avoir | Past participle | Complete phrase |
|---|---|---|---|
| j’ | ai | parlé | **j’ai parlé** |
| tu | as | fait | **tu as fait** |
| il | a | fini | **il a fini** |
| nous | avons | vendu | **nous avons vendu** |

---

# 9. Indirect Object Pronouns (IOPs)

An **indirect object pronoun** replaces an indirect object noun.

English example:

> Do you speak to your friend every day?  
> Yes, I speak to **him/her** every day.

French does the same thing, but the pronoun goes in a different place.

---

## IOP Chart

| French | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **me / m’** | to me |
| **te / t’** | to you |
| **lui** | to him / to her |
| **nous** | to us |
| **vous** | to you |
| **leur** | to them |

---

## Basic Word Order

Your lesson gives this formula:

> **Subject + IOP + Verb**

Example:

> **Je lui parle.**  
> I speak to him/her.

Breakdown:

| Word | Role |
|---|---|
| Je | subject |
| lui | indirect object pronoun |
| parle | verb |

---

## IOP Examples

| French | English |
|---|---|
| Tu me donnes un dollar. | You give me one dollar. |
| Je te dis quelque chose. | I say something to you. |
| Nous lui parlons souvent. | We often talk to him/her. |
| Il nous parle toujours. | He always talks to us. |
| Elle vous envoie un message. | She sends you a message. |
| Je leur prête ma voiture. | I lend them my car. |

# 13. Regular **-re** Verbs

A regular **-re** verb has an infinitive ending in **-re**.

Your lesson teaches this process:

1. Remove **-re** to find the stem.
2. Add the correct ending for the subject.

---

## Regular **-re** Endings

| Subject | Ending |
|---|---|
| je | **-s** |
| tu | **-s** |
| il / elle / on | **no ending** |
| nous | **-ons** |
| vous | **-ez** |
| ils / elles | **-ent** |

---

# 19. Beginner Grammar Map

When you see a French sentence, try identifying pieces in this order:

### Step 1 — Find the subject

Common subjects:

| French | English |
|---|---|
| je | I |
| tu | you |
| il | he |
| elle | she |
| on | one / we |
| nous | we |
| vous | you |
| ils | they |
| elles | they |

### Step 2 — Look for a small pronoun before the verb

Could be an indirect object pronoun:

> me, te, lui, nous, vous, leur

### Step 3 — Look for a form of **avoir**

> ai, as, a, avons, avez, ont

If a form of **avoir** is followed by a past participle, you may be looking at the **passé composé**.

### Step 4 — Look for a demonstrative adjective

> ce, cet, cette, ces

### Step 5 — Identify the main vocabulary word

Example:

> **Tu lui as donné ce cadeau.**

| Piece | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tu | you |
| lui | to him/her |
| as | have |
| donné | given |
| ce | this/that |
| cadeau | gift |

Overall meaning:

> **You gave him/her this gift.**
